title: What has happened to the Creative Industries?
summary: Some thoughts about craft, AI tools, the next generation and the soul of our industry.
published: 2026-11-02

We used to be the lefty, socially conscious and touchy feely people in the room. We were all concepts and consideration. The feels over the finance. Telling corporations that they needed to understand the real world value of their tone of voice.

And I get it, we need to pay the bills and keep the lights on. We needed to use the C-suite languages to be asked into the boardroom. But I am beginning to wonder that we have adopted this posture and drifted to a strange place where we are losing our souls.

My LinkedIn timeline is filled with ‘I smashed out this thing on Claude’ and my gut does a flip. What about the cost to the craft of design, if you can smash it out what’s to stop the CFO doing the same? What about the cost to the industry as a whole? The hollowing out of opportunity for a young designer to do that work. The environmental cost?

I’ve had graduates reach out to me saying that paid internships are on the decline. I’m seeing reports that the industry (and not just this industry) is asking for more experience at less pay.

I was at a conference last year and each speaker echoed the same message about AI tools, ‘Just have a play’. It made me itch a bit and at the end I asked, ‘what does that mean for the environment’? Should we just be spamming the prompt button, making endless mindless mistakes in the hope of creating ‘good enough’? Should we think about that? I was answered by a kind of dismissive, you just don’t get it. But what if we are making design worse?

My buddy Rose and I were chatting about this and she reminded me that the Luddites weren’t just indiscriminately smashing up looms because they hated the technology. Many of them were skilled machine operators. They weren’t against the machines, they were against owners using them to cut wages and turn out shoddier goods, and what that would do to an established industry.[^luddites]

This is not an AI essay. It’s some thoughts about the soul of our industry. I have often worried that we are not unionised, although I can’t really see what would happen if we all walked out... Less shelf wobblers? However without a union or a collective we can be an industry of insularity. Dreamers who are a bit too self absorbed.

Back in the 2000’s I was involved in a whole bunch of initiatives that were trying to elevate the Scottish Design Industry. The point was to get a seat at the table if independence happened. We wanted to remind any incoming government that the creative industries brings significant money into the economy and it should be supported and invested in.[^numbers]

I was also involved in community get togethers and, maybe because I was younger, I was always up for any creative event where we could all hang out. I met so many likeminded people. People who became life long friends. I got job opportunities and generally had a great time.[^longlunch]

Then I went away and came back. I didn’t really notice the change while I was away because I was online and I could still see people doing cool stuff and it felt like connection. But somewhere along the line it feels like we stopped caring about the next generation. We stopped caring about craft. We stopped caring about impact and the feels. We’ve retreated back into our bedrooms and are hiding our jotters. We’ve got nice houses and fancy cars and a seat at the table.

Does this even matter? Depends. If you’ve got a mortgage, kids and car payments and you have already made ‘bank’ as they say. I guess you can pull the ladder up. Feels quite far away from the creative industry I want to be part of.

If you care about what is next then you probably should. We are heading into an era of change and we are going to argue harder for craft, design thinking, the power of creativity to connect. We are going to need fresh eyes, new ways of making the argument and passionate people to help us defend an industry that has only just got its feet under the table.

[^luddites]: **The Luddites** Between 1811 and 1816 textile workers in the Midlands and the North broke machines, but they chose their targets. They went after owners who used frames to make cheap “cut-up” stockings or to undercut apprentice-trained workers, and often left other frames alone. Wages, food prices and the war with France fuelled the anger as much as the machines did. [Smithsonian Magazine](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/what-the-luddites-really-fought-against-264412/), [Kevin Binfield, Writings of the Luddites](https://campus.murraystate.edu/academic/faculty/kBinfield/luddites/LudditeHistory.htm)
